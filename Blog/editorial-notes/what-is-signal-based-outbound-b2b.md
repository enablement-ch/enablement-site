# Editorial note: What is signal-based outbound in B2B?

Drafted 2026-11-01. Public article file: `src/content/blog/what-is-signal-based-outbound-b2b.md`. The blog collection and routes are implemented, and the article is marked `draft: false` for publication.

## Original source and editorial choices

- Gilbert's 2026-11-01 voice note is the source of the three-level model: first-party engagement with the company, second-party category engagement, and third-party public business changes. It also supplies the volume tradeoff and the strategy of generating more first-party signals through content and distribution.
- Gilbert clarified that a signal is verifiable information that may indicate pain, need, interest, or intent. The article uses "verifiable observation" because first-party examples such as webinar signups and site visits are verifiable internally, but are not necessarily public.
- The three labels are framed as Enablement's operating model. Industry usage differs: some vendors call external category research third-party intent. This distinction is in the article so readers are not misled by the terminology.
- Claims that first-party is always highest intent and third-party is always worst were softened. A newsletter signup can be weak; a public event can be strong when it closely matches a known buyer problem. Volume is described as typical, not measured for Enablement.
- The project-management example is illustrative, not a customer result. No external vendor statistics were used.
