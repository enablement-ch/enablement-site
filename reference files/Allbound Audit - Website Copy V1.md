## The diagnostic

### The 10 places your GTM system quietly leaks revenue.

How many of these are happening inside your GTM system right now?

### 01 - GTM motion

**Pain:** Your market has fewer than 10,000 accounts. Your team is burning through it at a 0.2% positive reply rate.

**Impact:** Two or three BDRs and one or two AEs can spend up to 60% of their time researching companies and sending cold emails to enterprise buyers who have never heard of you. At a 0.2% to 0.3% positive reply rate, the team does not just waste salary and software budget. It burns through a finite market. The better motion builds trust through personal branding, retargets people who engage, combines the signals, and sends the hottest accounts into manual, multi-channel outreach. At Ohalo, that signal-ranked system generated $1.1 million in pipeline over 100 active campaign days, compared with $700,000 from two internal BDRs working in parallel.

**Diagnosis:** You did not need more outbound volume. You needed to stop treating a finite market like an infinite list.

### 02 - Customer intelligence

**Pain:** The founder says one ICP. Marketing says another. Sales targets a third.

**Impact:** The official ICP still lives in an agency workshop from two years ago. Marketing targets a broad audience while sales can only close a narrow part of it. Current evidence is buried in call recordings, CRM records, Slack threads, Google Docs, and individual reps' heads. Every rejected lead becomes another argument about quality. For one client, consolidating that evidence into shared account and buyer criteria brought list accuracy to 95% to 97% and increased conversion from marketing-qualified to sales-qualified leads by 30%.

**Diagnosis:** They did not need more leads. Marketing and sales needed one shared definition of a buyer worth pursuing.

### 03 - Tool architecture

**Pain:** Clay, Apollo, and HubSpot. Fifty clicks to move one lead into the CRM.

**Impact:** The team pays $500 to $800 a month for Clay, then uses it to find emails from LinkedIn Sales Navigator lists. Reps export CSV files between tools and manually fill CRM fields, turning expensive sales time into data entry. At Emporix, moving one lead into HubSpot took roughly three minutes and 50 clicks. After we connected the stack, it took under four seconds and pipeline output increased 3.2 times.

**Diagnosis:** A $500 tool used as an email finder is not infrastructure. A lean GTM stack only needs to do five jobs well: capture signals, enrich data, automate handoffs, run campaigns, and keep the CRM and reporting accurate.

### 04 - Targeting and timing

**Pain:** Every Monday, 50 more accounts enter the sequence. Nobody knows whether any are ready to buy.

**Impact:** The team works alphabetically through its target market, adds a job opening or funding round, and calls it intent. But those signals are public, late, and often unrelated to category interest. Every competitor with Apollo sees the same alert. While reps spend expensive hours chasing noise, the accounts visiting your website, downloading content, attending webinars, and engaging with your category pass through their buying window unnoticed. For CondenZero, conference-attendance signals helped identify 650 relevant people, book 22 meetings, and generate $2.4 million in pipeline.

**Diagnosis:** You do not need more signals. You need to score company fit and buyer readiness using the proximity, recency, and strength of each signal.

### 05 - Messaging and activation

**Pain:** You saw a job opening. Guessed the problem. Asked a stranger for a meeting.

**Impact:** "Hey, I saw you are hiring a data analyst. That usually means you have data-compliance problems. We have a solution. Want a demo?" The observation is real. Everything after it is an assumption. The role may be wrong, the problem may not exist, and the buyer has no reason to trust you. Yet the same message and hard meeting request go to every persona, segment, and stage of awareness. There is no useful asset or smaller next step for a buyer who is interested but not ready for a call. A cold account should not receive the same offer as someone who attended your webinar or repeatedly visited your website. Warmly replaced generic outreach with relevance-based messaging and moved responses from 0.2% to 7.7%, with 71% of responses classified as interested.

**Diagnosis:** The message was personalized. It was not relevant. Relevance comes from matching the buyer, signal, problem, awareness level, offer, and ask - from useful content for a cold buyer to a direct conversation for someone already showing intent.

### 06 - Data and qualification

**Pain:** Your CRM says 10,000 contacts. By next quarter, 1,000 to 2,000 could already be wrong.

**Impact:** People change jobs, get promoted, leave companies, and change email addresses. Duplicates and incomplete records keep entering while the team continues working from a database that no longer reflects reality. Nobody has clearly defined which companies and contacts should never enter the motion. Sales reps manually repair fields because automations either fail to trigger or trigger incorrectly. Reporting becomes impossible to trust. Then a customer, partner, or active opportunity receives a cold sequence because automated campaigns and sales reps are working from different lists. One message can make the company look disconnected, upset a customer, or put a live deal at risk.

**Diagnosis:** Bad data does not stay in the CRM. It reaches buyers. Define the exclusion rules, then gate, enrich, deduplicate, and synchronize every record before it can enter outreach, automation, or reporting.

### 07 - Lead qualification and routing

**Pain:** More than 300 positive replies in three months. Only 10% to 15% became meetings.

**Impact:** A form fill, download, event attendee, or positive reply is not automatically a sales-qualified lead. But waiting for a rep to inspect every record manually is equally expensive. Blueprint thought outbound was not working because positive replies sat across disconnected tools, were not followed up consistently, and reached HubSpot without the context or attribution sales needed. Once account and contact qualification, routing, follow-up, and attribution were fixed, conversion from positive reply to initial meeting reached 50%. The outbound motion went on to contribute more than $10 million in closed revenue.

**Diagnosis:** Generating demand was not the problem. Catching it was. Qualify interest at the account and contact level, then give every sales-ready lead complete context, an owner, an immediate notification, and a defined response path.

### 08 - Sales process and automation

**Pain:** The CRM says the quarter is covered. Nobody can say which deals will actually close.

**Impact:** The sales call ends without a structured summary, committed next meeting, or clear action. The rep switches tasks and later updates whichever CRM fields they remember. Lifecycle stages do not match, and deal stages have no defined entry or exit criteria. A deal moves forward because someone changed a dropdown, not because the buyer made a commitment. The forecast looks healthy, but it is built on stale stages, missing context, and rep optimism. The revenue gap becomes visible only when it is too late to recover the quarter. Meanwhile, the most expensive people on the team are preparing meetings, writing summaries, updating records, and setting reminders by hand.

**Diagnosis:** Automate the administration around the sale, not the buyer conversation. Reps should build relationships and close deals. The system should prepare context, capture outcomes, enforce stage criteria, trigger follow-up, and produce an evidence-based forecast.

### 09 - Revenue reporting

**Pain:** One GTM motion was outperforming another by 3.7 times. The dashboard made them look the same.

**Impact:** The client believed both motions were creating similar results because reporting stopped at aggregate contacts, replies, meetings, and deals. Once attribution connected each lead from first touch through every lifecycle stage to closed revenue, one motion was producing 3.7 times more. Without that view, leadership could have hired into the weaker channel, cut the stronger one, or scaled a funnel that was still leaking revenue.

**Diagnosis:** A dashboard that cannot change an investment decision is reporting theatre. Leadership needs conversion and revenue by channel, every week and every month, so it knows what to scale, iterate, fix, or kill.

### 10 - Feedback and experimentation

**Pain:** The campaign ends. Someone says, "I do not like the copy." Four variables change on Monday.

**Impact:** There was no hypothesis, control, or agreed success metric. The team changes the audience, message, offer, and channel at the same time, then runs too little volume to know whether anything improved. A deal closes and the commission is collected. Another is lost and marked closed-lost. Nobody asks what the wins had in common, why the losses happened, or what the calls revealed. The next idea comes from gut feeling, a competitor, a friend, or whatever is popular on LinkedIn that week. Promising segments are killed before they produce enough evidence. Weak ideas continue because one reply felt encouraging. Eventually, the team concludes that the channel does not work without ever having tested it properly.

**Diagnosis:** Without a learning loop, every campaign starts from zero and every decision becomes another opinion. Test one hypothesis with enough volume, then feed replies, sales calls, wins, and losses back into targeting, scoring, messaging, and content. Run the loop weekly while building the system, monthly while improving it, and quarterly once revenue becomes reliable.

### What this adds up to

More tools create more activity. More activity does not automatically create more pipeline. If your GTM system is poorly designed, the only things you scale are manual work, disconnected data, and cost.

## Two operating models

### From disconnected activities to one Allbound System.

The difference is not another tool. It is what happens between the tools you already have.

| The old world | The new world |
|---|---|
| Teams choose channels from habit and disagree on the buyer | One evidence-based motion aligns the team around how buyers buy |
| Disconnected tools and dirty data create manual work | A lean, connected stack keeps data clean and work automated |
| Static lists and generic messages treat every buyer the same | Fit, intent, and awareness determine the next best action |
| Qualified interest gets lost between marketing, sales, and the CRM | Every qualified lead has context, an owner, and a path to revenue |
| Reports show activity while decisions rely on gut feeling | Revenue attribution and feedback improve every next decision |

**Old-world verdict:** More activity. More tools. No clear path to revenue.

**New-world verdict:** Every stage connects. Every outcome improves the next decision.

## Before you book

### What to expect from the Allbound Audit.

#### What happens during the session?

Lanny maps your go-to-market funnel from first activity to closed revenue. Together, you work through the numbers behind your inbound, outbound, partnerships, lead management, sales process, and reporting. The goal is to identify where opportunities and revenue are actually being lost, not to give you another list of generic best practices.

#### How long does it take, and what does it cost?

The session is completely free. Plan for 30 minutes. We leave up to 45 minutes when the system needs a deeper look.

#### What should I prepare?

Bring the best numbers you have: current marketing activities, inbound, outbound and partnership performance, top-line revenue, average contract value, lead volume, reply rates, meetings booked, qualified meetings, opportunities, closed-won deals, and your typical sales cycle. Imperfect numbers are fine. Missing numbers are often part of the diagnosis.

#### Do I need to share my CRM?

No. We can work with the numbers you provide. If you want to open your CRM or dashboards during the session, that usually makes the diagnosis faster and more precise.

#### What do I receive afterward?

You receive a written diagnosis within 24 hours. It captures the bottlenecks we found, the evidence behind them, the order in which they should be addressed, and what your team can fix internally.

#### Who is the Allbound Audit for?

It is designed for B2B SaaS and tech-enabled service companies with product-market fit, usually between $1M and $15M in annual revenue. The strongest fit is a company that has no repeatable outbound system or has already invested in outbound, tools, or headcount without getting reliable pipeline from it. It is not designed for pre-product-market-fit companies or businesses below $500K in annual revenue.

#### What if Enablement is not the right solution?

You still receive the diagnosis. Lanny will explain what should be fixed internally and where your team should focus next. If the problem is one we can solve, he will also explain what working together could look like.
