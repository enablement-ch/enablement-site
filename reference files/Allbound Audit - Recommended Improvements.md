# Allbound Audit - Recommended Improvements

**Purpose:** Independent conversion and positioning review of the current landing-page draft

**Current page:** `https://enablement.ch/resources/allbound-audit/`

---

# Executive View

The page has the right core idea: most teams do not need another campaign or tool. They need to find where intent, context, leads, and revenue disappear between systems.

The strongest parts are:

- The contrarian hero premise
- The ten recognizable failure points
- The old-world versus new-world comparison
- The amount of available customer proof
- The connection between the audit and Enablement's actual service

The biggest weakness is that the audit itself is still less concrete than the problem.

By the time someone reaches the CTA, they understand that their architecture might be broken. But they still do not know exactly what they are applying for, how much effort it requires, what they receive, or why this audit is better than an ordinary discovery call.

The next iteration should make the audit feel like a valuable product in its own right, not a renamed sales call.

---

# My Main Recommendation

Position this as a **free, qualification-gated, 45-minute Allbound Architecture Audit** for B2B teams with either no outbound motion or a motion that is producing activity without reliable pipeline.

The visitor should receive:

1. A visual bottleneck map of their current motion
2. Their three highest-impact failure points
3. A recommended build order for what to fix first
4. A clear answer on whether to repair the current system, rebuild it, or leave it alone

Send a short written summary after the call. It does not need to be a large bespoke report. A standardized one-page template is enough.

This is the most important change because it turns the CTA from "book a conversation" into "receive a useful diagnosis."

---

# Priority 1 - Lock the Offer Before Rewriting More Copy

The page cannot become fully persuasive until these decisions are fixed.

## Recommended Offer Definition

**Name:** Allbound Architecture Audit

**Price:** Free

**Access:** Application required

**Duration:** 45 minutes

**Preparation:** Five-minute intake form covering current channels, tools, team, pipeline target, and biggest concern

**Call structure:**

1. Map the current motion
2. Identify the highest-impact leaks
3. Prioritize the repair or build order
4. Decide whether Enablement should help execute

**Post-call deliverable:** One-page bottleneck map and prioritized recommendation

**Fit criteria:**

- B2B SaaS, technology-enabled services, consultancies, or agencies
- Roughly $1M-$15M revenue or ARR
- Founder, CEO, Head of Sales, or another person with budget and GTM ownership
- No repeatable outbound system, or an existing motion that is underperforming
- A real growth target or forcing function in the next six months

**Disqualify:**

- People only researching GTM tooling
- Teams looking for isolated Clay support
- Sophisticated internal GTM teams seeking small optimization gains
- Companies without a defined offer or any evidence of product-market fit

## Why Free and Qualification-Gated

Charging for the audit creates friction before enough trust exists. Making it completely open creates low-quality calendar volume. A free application creates value while preserving control over who receives the time.

# Priority 2 - Make the Hero More Concrete

The current headline is good but still broad:

> Your pipeline does not need more activity. It needs fewer leaks.

It creates tension, but it does not tell the visitor exactly what the audit does or who it is for.

## Recommended Hero Direction

**Eyebrow:** Free Allbound Architecture Audit

### Find where your GTM engine is losing pipeline before you add another tool, campaign, or hire.

In one working session, we map how accounts move from intent to revenue, identify the three bottlenecks costing you the most pipeline, and show you what to fix first.

**Primary CTA:** Apply for your Allbound Audit

**Supporting line:** 45 minutes. One bottleneck map. A prioritized build order.

## Alternative Headline

### You do not have a lead problem. You have ten places where good leads disappear.

This version is more provocative and connects directly to the checklist, but it is less accurate for teams that genuinely lack lead volume. The first recommendation is safer and more commercially precise.

# Priority 3 - Put Proof Immediately After the Promise

The logo belt is good. Keep it directly under the hero.

However, logos only answer "Have credible companies trusted you?" They do not answer "Will this system create commercial results?"

Add a compact three-result strip between the VSL and the ten-point diagnostic.

## Recommended Proof Strip

### $10M+

Closed revenue supported by Blueprint's global outbound system

### 2x

Seven-figure pipeline for Bound within 90 days

### 8-10x

Meeting volume for Skillfit.io

These three cases cover:

- Large commercial outcome
- Fast pipeline outcome
- Relative improvement from a weak starting point

Do not put six full case cards near the top. The early proof should be fast and numerical. Keep the fuller case-study section later on the page.

# Priority 4 - Turn the Checklist Into a Self-Diagnosis

The ten pain points are the page's strongest sales mechanism. Right now they read as content. They should feel like a diagnosis.

## Recommended Interaction

Keep the visible checkboxes and allow the visitor to select each problem that applies.

After the tenth item, calculate a simple result:

- **0-2 selected:** Your system is likely functional. You may need optimization rather than a rebuild.
- **3-5 selected:** Your pipeline is leaking across several handoffs. Fixing them in the right order matters.
- **6-10 selected:** More activity will amplify the waste. Map the architecture before launching another campaign.

Then transition directly into the audit CTA:

> Selected three or more? We will map the exact failure points and tell you what to fix first.

## If We Want to Keep the First Version Static

Add a line above the list:

> Count every statement that is true for your team. Three or more means the issue is probably structural, not tactical.

This creates participation without requiring JavaScript.

# Priority 5 - Raise the Business Pain, Not Only the Operational Pain

Several checklist items currently describe workflow inconvenience. Buyers pay to solve revenue, capacity, and risk problems.

Each impact statement should connect the operational fault to a commercial consequence.

## Examples

### Current

> Bad records compound across enrichment, routing, reporting, and rep follow-up.

### Stronger

> Reps work duplicate accounts, attribution becomes unreliable, and leadership makes budget decisions from numbers nobody trusts.

### Current

> Seller time gets consumed by inbox administration instead of conversations.

### Stronger

> Your most expensive people spend the week clearing noise while high-intent buyers wait for a response.

### Current

> Your best opportunities disappear inside a list built for volume.

### Stronger

> High-intent accounts receive the same generic treatment as everyone else, so the team wastes the buying window.

The operational problem creates recognition. The commercial consequence creates urgency.

# Priority 6 - Show the Audit Deliverable

The outcome section currently names three outputs, but the visitor cannot picture them.

Create a visual mockup of the one-page audit result:

- A ten-point architecture score
- Red, amber, and green bottleneck status
- The top three priority failures
- Estimated operational impact
- Recommended sequence: fix now, fix next, leave alone

The visual can be explicitly labeled as an example. Its purpose is to make the outcome tangible.

This will likely improve conversion more than another paragraph of copy.

# Priority 7 - Tighten the Old World Versus New World Section

The current comparison is directionally right, but some rows describe implementation details rather than business outcomes.

Use paired contrasts that show what changes for the buyer.

| Old World | Allbound System |
|---|---|
| Sales works whatever list was exported last | Sales opens a ranked queue of in-market accounts |
| Every prospect receives the same sequence | Effort and messaging change with fit and intent |
| Positive replies wait in separate inboxes | Every response has an owner and response SLA |
| The CRM stores incomplete outcomes | Signal, source, research, reply, and revenue stay connected |
| Reporting ends at replies and meetings | Leadership sees the path from signal to closed-won |
| A good month cannot be explained or repeated | The team knows what worked and can run it again |

The final bridge should be:

> The difference is not more activity. It is an operating system that tells every signal what happens next.

# Priority 8 - Use Three Primary Case Studies, Then Offer More

Six case studies are useful, but six equal cards create too much visual competition.

I recommend featuring three cases with a specific narrative role:

## Blueprint - Scale and Revenue

- $10M+ closed revenue
- 30-40 qualified meetings per month
- High eight-figure pipeline
- Global motion across 30 countries

**Story it proves:** A connected system can scale a complex market-expansion motion.

## Bound - Speed and Repair

- Doubled seven-figure pipeline in 90 days
- Lean revenue team
- Structured targeting, messaging, execution, and HubSpot reporting

**Story it proves:** A manual, inconsistent motion can become measurable quickly.

## Skillfit.io - Before and After

- From one meeting every two weeks to 4-5 per week
- 8-10x meeting volume
- System stayed with the team

**Story it proves:** A small B2B team can build capability without creating agency dependency.

After these three, use a simple link:

**CTA:** See three more customer results

This lets interested visitors expand into CondenZero, Ekipa, and Lipdub without making the main argument feel endless.

# Priority 9 - Add Qualification Before the Final CTA

The current fit line is too broad:

> For B2B teams with no outbound motion or one that is not producing.

Add a two-column fit section.

## This Audit Is for You If

- You have reached the limit of founder-led sales, referrals, or inbound alone.
- You own several GTM tools but still cannot produce reliable pipeline.
- Your team is active, but you cannot explain where opportunities disappear.
- You need a repeatable outbound system without hiring a full internal GTM engineering team.
- You have a real growth target in the next six months.

## This Is Not the Right Audit If

- You only want a list of tools.
- You need help with one isolated Clay table.
- You have no proven offer or customer traction yet.
- You are collecting ideas without a timeline to change anything.

This will reduce low-fit bookings and increase the credibility of the offer.

# Priority 10 - Add a Short FAQ

The page needs to answer the objections that stop someone from applying.

## Recommended Questions

### Is this a sales call?

The audit is a working session. We will tell you where the motion is breaking and what we would fix first. If we believe Enablement can help execute, we will explain how. If not, you still leave with the diagnosis.

### What do I need to prepare?

Complete the short intake and bring your current GTM stack, channels, team structure, and pipeline target. Screenshots are enough. You do not need to prepare a formal presentation.

### Who should join?

The founder, CEO, Head of Sales, or person who owns both the pipeline target and the authority to change the system.

### What do I receive afterwards?

A one-page map of the highest-impact bottlenecks and a recommended order for what to fix first.

### What if we already use Clay, HubSpot, Apollo, or a sequencing platform?

That is normal. The audit is not about replacing tools. It examines whether signals, data, messaging, routing, and revenue reporting work as one system.

### What if we have no outbound motion yet?

We map what needs to exist, what should be built first, and which infrastructure can wait until the motion is proven.

# Recommended VSL Direction

The reference VSL has a strong structure, but Enablement should not copy its aggressive income framing or enormous volume of proof.

The Allbound VSL should be approximately 8-10 minutes.

## Recommended Sequence

### 00:00-00:30 - Diagnosis

Most teams think they need more leads, another campaign, or another tool. But pipeline usually dies between the systems they already have.

### 00:30-01:15 - Promise

Explain that the audit maps the path from intent to revenue, identifies the three highest-impact leaks, and gives the team a build order.

### 01:15-02:15 - Proof

Use Blueprint, Bound, and Skillfit. One result and one sentence per case.

### 02:15-03:45 - Pain Scenes

Use three recognizable moments:

1. A positive reply sits unnoticed.
2. Sales receives a lead without the context that made the person respond.
3. Leadership sees replies and meetings but no attributable closed pipeline.

### 03:45-05:30 - The Mechanism

Explain three layers:

1. Signal and prioritization
2. Activation and routing
3. CRM context and revenue reporting

### 05:30-06:30 - Cost of Inaction

Show what it costs when sellers research manually, high-intent accounts receive generic outreach, and pipeline cannot be traced.

### 06:30-07:30 - What Happens in the Audit

Show the intake, working session, bottleneck map, and prioritized recommendation.

### 07:30-08:30 - Qualification and CTA

Explain who it is for, who it is not for, and ask the visitor to apply.

## Recommended VSL Opening

> If your team is sending messages, booking some meetings, and still cannot explain where the pipeline is going, you probably do not have a campaign problem. You have an architecture problem.
>
> Somewhere between intent, targeting, enrichment, outreach, routing, and the CRM, good opportunities are disappearing. And adding more volume only makes the leak more expensive.
>
> In the Allbound Architecture Audit, we map that entire path, identify the three bottlenecks costing you the most pipeline, and show you what to fix first.

# Copy Issues to Fix

## Use One Name Consistently

Choose either:

- Allbound Audit
- Allbound Architecture Audit

I recommend **Allbound Architecture Audit** because it makes the object of the audit explicit and separates it from a generic sales or funnel audit.

## Replace Abstract Language

Avoid phrases such as:

- Connected view
- Current motion
- Practical diagnosis
- Highest-impact improvements

Use visible outputs instead:

- A map of where leads disappear
- The three bottlenecks costing the most pipeline
- The order in which to fix them
- The tools and workflows to leave alone

## Reduce Repetition

The current draft repeats variants of "what is broken and what to fix first" several times. Keep this as the core promise, but vary what each section contributes:

- Hero: the outcome
- Checklist: recognition
- Old versus new: the belief shift
- Audit process: how it happens
- Deliverable: what they receive
- Proof: why they should trust us
- Final CTA: who should act now

# What I Would Not Add Yet

- A long tools logo cloud
- Detailed technical architecture diagrams above the fold
- Pricing for the Fractional GTME engagement
- More than six case studies
- A long founder biography
- A complex calculator
- A downloadable PDF before the audit CTA
- A chatbot or automated assessment

These can add weight later, but they distract from the first conversion goal: qualify and book the right audit.

---

# Recommended Iteration Order

## Round 1 - Offer

Lock the audit duration, qualification, intake, and post-call deliverable.

## Round 2 - Copy

Rewrite the hero, checklist impacts, comparison, qualification section, and FAQ around the locked offer.

## Round 3 - Proof

Choose the three primary cases and create the compact metric strip.

## Round 4 - Visual Deliverable

Design the example bottleneck map shown on the landing page and used after real audits.

## Round 5 - VSL

Write and record the VSL after the page promise and offer mechanics are final. The VSL should sell the same product described on the page.

## Round 6 - Conversion Setup

Create a dedicated booking or application flow, preserve attribution, and define follow-up after application and after the audit.

---

# Bottom Line

The page does not need more content. It needs a sharper product.

Make the audit specific, show the deliverable, quantify the proof early, and let the ten-point checklist do the persuasion. Once those pieces are locked, the VSL becomes much easier to write because it has a concrete promise, mechanism, and outcome to sell.
